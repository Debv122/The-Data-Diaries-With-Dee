Why this exists

I teach the way I wish I'd been taught: one idea at a time, with a file open in front of you. Every lesson ends with something you make, not something you watch.

The running order isn't my opinion. I took an analysis of 328 real data analyst job descriptions and taught the most-demanded skills first, so if you stop halfway you've still learned the things you're most likely to be asked about in an interview.

Everything lives in one place, lessons, exercise files, and the worked solutions, so you can come back to a technique two months later and find it in under a minute.

Questions

The things people ask before buying.

Do I need any experience to start?
No. The analyst course assumes you can open a spreadsheet and type in a cell, and nothing beyond that. Every module opens with a short primer covering the knowledge it assumes, which you can skip if you already have it.
Why is Excel taught before SQL and Python?
Because that is the order employers ask for them in. Across 328 analyst job descriptions this year, Excel appeared in 81%, SQL in 60%, Power BI in 43% and Python in 41%. If you stop halfway through the course you have still learned the two things you are most likely to be tested on.
What software do I need?
Excel or Google Sheets, Power BI Desktop, and Python. All three are free to use for the course, and the sample database is hosted, so there is nothing to install to follow the SQL modules.
How long does it take?
It depends which track you take. Twelve weeks at three hours a week if you are starting cold, six to eight weeks self-paced if you already work with data, or a fixed eight-week cohort for teams. Same lessons in all three, different pacing and assessment.
Is the AI course only for technical people?
No, and it involves no coding. It is aimed at whoever owns a process that is eating the week. The hard parts are choosing the right process, measuring a baseline before you build, and deciding where a human stays in the loop.
Do I get feedback on the capstone?
On the all-access and team plans, yes, marked against the published rubric. On a single course you get the rubric and the worked example, and you can mark it yourself.
What if it isn't for me?
Email within 30 days and I will refund you, no forms and no questions. The first three modules are free, so watch those before you buy and you probably will not need to.
